# KinShip identity numbers (Nov 16, 2026)

Asked by JC: a person should be able to carry a national ID, for example the **Cédula** in the Dominican Republic or the **CURP** in Mexico. For deceased relatives the number helps when requesting records from the state (civil registry, archives, consulates). Companion to `kinship-v2-requirements.md`, `kinship-research-records-and-backup.md`, `kinship-storage-encryption.md` and `kinship-adoption.md`. Anything marked *(assumption)* is my default, not yet confirmed by JC.

**Decided by JC, Nov 16:** (1) add a **Records request sheet**; (2) a deceased person's numbers are visible to their **immediate relatives: parents, siblings and children**; (3) **field-level encryption is in v1.0** (my recommendation, accepted); (4) the starter list covers the **US, Mexico, the Dominican Republic, Canada and 2 to 3 European countries**; (5) **a legally married spouse also sees them, an unmarried Partner does not**; (6) **admins see a deceased person's numbers, a Separated spouse counts (still legally married), half siblings count, and no confirmation is asked when a marriage status changes**; (7) **who can edit: the person while living; after death an admin or next of kin**; (8) **a birth, marriage or death record number is not an identity number: it belongs on that fact's source** (see below); (9) **for an adopted person the adoptive family is the legal family and the next of kin; birth relatives do not count automatically** (decided later on Nov 16; see `kinship-adoption.md`).

**In the mockup (Nov 16):** the Facts tab has an Identity numbers section, Edit details has a panel pointing to it, and Archive → Reports has the Records request sheet in English and Spanish. A "preview as" selector shows what each person would see. All numbers are fictional. **The mockup was built before decision 9 and still counts birth relatives of an adopted person.**

## What it is
A section on the profile **Facts** tab, **Identity numbers**, and a matching panel in **Edit details**. A person can have **several** entries, because many people have more than one number or an old and a new format. **An identity number is a number issued to the person** (Cédula, CURP, Social Security number). The number of a birth, marriage or death certificate is a number issued to the **record**, so it goes in the **page or record number** of the citation on that fact (birth, death or a marriage event), where the source and any scan already sit (decided Nov 16).

Each entry has:
- **Country** (required, picked from a list).
- **Type** (required): a seeded list per country, plus **Other with your own label**. A new label joins the shared list, like custom conditions, and admins can merge or rename.
- **Number** (required).
- **Issued** and **Expires** (optional, flexible dates), **issuing place** (optional, from the Places library) and a **note** (for example "number on the 1985 card; the new card differs"). The mockup has issued and note; expires and issuing place are still to add.
- **Sources & files**, the same panel as birth and death, so a scan of the card or a civil registry record can be attached.

**Starter list (decided: the countries; the exact types and the European picks are my proposal):**
- **United States:** Social Security number; Alien registration number.
- **Mexico:** CURP; Clave de elector (INE); RFC.
- **Dominican Republic:** Cédula de Identidad y Electoral.
- **Canada:** Social Insurance Number.
- **Europe, 3 picks *(assumption, easy to swap)*:** **Spain** (DNI, NIE), **Italy** (Codice fiscale), **United Kingdom** (National Insurance number). Chosen for common emigration to the Americas and the UK; JC to name different ones if his family came from elsewhere.
- **No "Civil registry reference" type (removed Nov 16).** Record or acta numbers go on the source of the birth, death or marriage fact. The Records request sheet picks them up from there and prints them next to the source, so an office still gets the number it asks for first.
- Any other country or type: **Other with your own label**.

**Format hints, never blocks.** A type may carry a hint and a soft warning, for example a CURP has 18 characters and a Dominican cédula has 11 digits. A number that does not match shows a yellow note and saves anyway, because old and foreign numbers vary. Hints to be checked against each country's official format before building.

## Who can see it
Identity numbers are the most sensitive field in the app (identity theft), so they do not follow the "visible to every account" rule used for DNA and cause of death.
- **Living person (assumption):** visible only to that person. **Admins have no special access**, the same rule as medical records. A living person may share an entry with specific people who have accounts.
- **Deceased person (decided by JC, Nov 16):** visible to the person's **parents, siblings, children and legally married spouse, if they have accounts**. **Half siblings count** (decided Nov 16). Everyone else, including Members who are not close relatives, and Users, does not see the numbers.
  - **Legally married spouse (decided by JC, Nov 16):** only a spouse, never an unmarried partner. A relationship counts as a legal marriage when its status is **Married** or **Widowed** (the surviving spouse of the deceased, the usual next of kin for records). **Separated also counts (decided Nov 16),** because people who are separated are still legally married. **Not counted:** Partner, Engaged, Friends, Unknown, Other, and the ended statuses **Divorced** and **Annulment**. The status is the existing relationship status, so nobody has to say it twice. An admin can correct the status if it was entered wrongly, and the change is in the change history.
  - **Why it needs care:** a Partner can be a long-term companion, but the app has no way to tell a legal marriage from an informal one except the status, so the rule is only as good as the status. The relationship card should say so when a status is changed to or from a counted one: "A legally married spouse can see this person's identity numbers after death."
  - **Admins (decided Nov 16):** admins see a deceased person's numbers, because they edit everything on the tree and run records requests. Admins still have no access to a **living** person's numbers.
  - **Adopted people (decided by JC, Nov 16, later):** **the adoptive family is the legal family and the next of kin.** A person's **legal parents** are their adoptive parents if they have any, otherwise their birth parents. For a deceased adopted person the next of kin are their **adoptive parents, adoptive siblings, children, a legally married spouse and admins**. **Birth relatives of an adopted person do not count automatically**, and there is no Private flag (JC removed it). Conversely, a child who was adopted away is not a child of their birth parent for this purpose. **Step relatives, in-laws, grandchildren and a spouse's relatives do not count.** The rule reads the **link kinds** (adoptive and birth), never the viewer's display setting. Foster and guardian relationships stay out. Open: step-parent adoption (see `kinship-adoption.md`).
  - Relatives and the spouse are found from the tree, so each must be linked on the tree and have an account.
- **Marking someone deceased** switches their numbers from the "only me" rule to this rule, with the same kind of toast used for medical history (the mockup's Edit details note says so). Marking them living again switches back. Marking someone deceased also turns their Married relationships to Widowed, so the surviving spouse keeps access.
- **Who can write (decided Nov 16):** **while the person is living, only that person** (their own linked account, whatever its role; admins cannot). **After death, an admin or next of kin** (the same people who can see the numbers: legal parents, legal siblings, children and legally married spouse, with accounts). Users with no account cannot. Edits are written to the change history.
- Enforced with **row-level security** in Postgres, copying the medical policies, and covered by the role-by-role privacy test suite. The test suite must include a Partner, an Engaged couple, a Divorced ex-spouse and a Separated spouse for the same deceased person, an adoptive sibling, **a birth relative of an adopted person (must not see the numbers)**, and a check that an admin cannot read or edit a living person's numbers.

## How it is shown and protected
- **Masked by default** (the last four characters visible, for example `•••-••-6789`), with a **Reveal** button and a **Copy** button. Revealing and copying are written to the change history. *(assumption)* Built in the mockup.
- **Field-level encryption, in v1.0 (decided).** Numbers are encrypted in the database with their own key (`IDENTIFIER_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, with `IDENTIFIER_ENCRYPTION_KEY_FILE` for Docker secrets). The key lives in `.env` (mode 600) or Docker secrets, never in the database or the Admin page, and is **not included in backups**, like `FILE_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and the backup passphrase. Losing it makes the numbers unreadable, so the deployer must store it separately. The API decrypts only after the row-level security check passes. Because the numbers are encrypted, they **cannot be searched or matched in SQL**; duplicate hints use a keyed hash of country, type and number kept beside the encrypted value. Admin page shows read-only status (key present, entries encrypted) and a **key rotation** command, later. Details to settle with `kinship-storage-encryption.md`.
- **Never** appears in: emails and notifications, the tree, the relationship finder, URLs, logs, the search box, or any report **except** the Records request sheet and an explicit "include identity numbers" box on other reports (off by default), and then only for what the viewer may see.
- **GEDCOM export leaves them out by default**, with a checkbox to include them. On import, GEDCOM identity fields (IDNO, SSN) are brought in as entries, flagged for review. *(assumption)*
- **Duplicate merge** may use an identical number plus country and type as a hint ("Same Cédula as Danny"), shown to admins only and only when the viewer may see both numbers. It never merges automatically. When people are merged, entries are kept as a list and an exact duplicate is collapsed.
- Not required for anyone. A person with no entries shows "No identity numbers on record".
- The profile section has a **Who can see these numbers** list that names the people who can see this person's numbers right now, says who is not counted and why (for example "Lucia Ortiz (divorced)"), and says that step relatives and in-laws do not see them. Built in the mockup.

## Records request sheet (decided by JC, Nov 16)
A one-page letter and fact sheet for **requesting a deceased relative's records from a state or other office**, made from a fixed template (no AI). Opened from a deceased person's profile (**Records request sheet** button in the Identity numbers section) and from **Archive → Reports**.

**Who can make it:** any account, for a **deceased** person only. A living person has no sheet. Identity numbers appear **only if the viewer may see them** (legal parent, sibling, child or legally married spouse of the deceased, or an admin); otherwise the sheet is made without them and says so. The sheet lists nothing the viewer cannot already see.

**What it holds:**
- **The person:** full name, with maiden name, married surname and other names the tree knows (important because registries file under the birth name, and Hispanic names carry two surnames); sex; birth date and place; death date and place; cause of death (checkbox, off by default).
- **Family:** father's and mother's full names (mother's maiden name; for an adopted person both the birth and the adoptive parents, see `kinship-adoption.md`), spouse or spouses (married, widowed, separated, divorced or annulled, with the marriage date; an unmarried partner is not listed as a spouse), and children's names (children's names off by default; living minors never).
- **Identity numbers:** each number the viewer may see, with its country and type, **each with its own checkbox, on by default**. A warning says the printout is as sensitive as the number itself and suggests sending it only to the office.
- **Sources already on file:** the attached sources and files (title, **record number from the citation**, reliability, repository), so the office or the researcher sees what is already known and gets the certificate number.
- **Records wanted** (checkboxes): birth record, death record, marriage record, identity-card record (for example Cédula or CURP file), military record, immigration or naturalization record, burial record, census record. Plus a free-text line.
- **Requester block:** the viewer's name, **relationship to the deceased** (worked out by the relationship finder, for example "Son", "Wife" for a widow, "Grandson"), a **purpose** (family history, legal or estate, citizenship, other), and blank lines for address, phone, email and signature, plus the date. A spouse is named as the husband or wife, which is the relationship most offices expect.
- **Office and country hints:** pick the country and the sheet shows a suggested office to write to and a note that offices differ and may need proof of relationship or a death certificate. Hints are **editable text, not live lookups**, and need checking against each office's current rules before building. Examples to verify: Dominican Republic (the Oficialía del Estado Civil under the Junta Central Electoral), Mexico (the state or local Registro Civil, and RENAPO for CURP matters), United States (the state vital records office, or the NYC Department of Health or Municipal Archives for New York City), Canada (the province's vital statistics office), Spain (Registro Civil), Italy (the comune's stato civile), United Kingdom (General Register Office). A state or province choice for the US and Canada is still to add. *(assumption)*
- **Language:** the letter can be printed in **English or Spanish** (all text as translation keys, so other languages can be added by the community). Names and numbers are never translated. *(assumption: Spanish at launch because of the Dominican and Mexican use cases)*

**Output:** print or save as PDF, and **Copy as text** for pasting into an email or an office's web form. Word follows with the other Word reports in 1.1. Options are hidden when printing.

**Safeguards:** creating or copying a sheet that includes identity numbers is written to the change history like a Reveal. Nothing is stored beyond that; the sheet is made on screen each time.

## Data model and build notes
- New table `person_identifier` (id, person_id, country, identifier_type_id, number_encrypted, number_lookup_hash, issued_at as text plus precision, expires_at as text plus precision, place_id nullable, note, created_by, created_at, updated_at).
- New shared table `identifier_type` (id, country, name, format_hint, is_custom, created_by), seeded with the starter list; same merge and rename tooling as `condition_type`.
- A share table for per-person sharing of a living person's entries, modelled on the medical sharing table.
- Row-level security copies `person_condition` for own person and shared-with-viewer. For a deceased person it adds a policy that allows a viewer who is the person's **legal parent, legal sibling or child on the tree, or their legally married spouse, or an admin**. That needs a database function, `is_immediate_relative_or_spouse(viewer_person, subject_person)`, which the medical and DNA policies do not use, so it gets its own tests. The spouse branch reads the relationship status, true for **Married, Widowed and Separated** and false for everything else, including **Partner, Engaged, Divorced and Annulment**. **The relative branch reads the parent-child link kinds: a person's legal parents are their adoptive parents if any, otherwise their birth parents (decided Nov 16); siblings are people who share a legal parent; children are people for whom the subject is a legal parent.** A second function, `can_edit_identifier`, allows the person themself while living, and an admin or the same relatives after death. The function lives in one place so the Records request sheet, the profile list of who can see the numbers, and the policy all use the same rule.
- Changes write to the change history (never storing the number itself, only "Cédula added", "changed", "revealed", "included in a records request").
- Translation keys for type names, hints, and the whole Records request letter in English and Spanish.
- **Mockup, built Nov 16 (fictional numbers only):** a Facts section with masked numbers, Reveal and Copy; an Add number window with country, type, format warning, issued and note; an Edit details panel; the **Who can see these numbers** list; the Records request sheet report in English and Spanish with per-number checkboxes and a Copy as text button; and a **preview as** selector (Marcus, Rosa, Daniel, Carla, Ines, Sofia, Beatriz, Lucas). Walter's certificate number is shown as the detail of his **birth source** ("Certificate no. 4412"), not as an identity number, and appears on the sheet's sources list. What it shows for **Walter** (died 2009): Rosa, his widow (Widowed, counted as spouse), Daniel and Carla (children) and Ines (his daughter from the Divorced marriage to Lucia) see his numbers; **Lucia is listed as not counted (divorced)**; Marcus, a grandchild, sees them only through his admin role; Sofia, Beatriz and Lucas do not. For **Lucia**, Ines sees them. Rosa's own numbers are private to her while she lives. There is no Partner or Engaged couple in the sample family, so the exclusion of a Partner is shown by the explanation text, not by a sample person.
